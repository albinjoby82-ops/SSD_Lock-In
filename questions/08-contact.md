Two materials have filled and empty levels set by E_F1 (low) and E_F2 (high). Re-draw the filled and empty levels when they're brought into contact.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Electrons from material 2 drop into the empty levels of material 1.

E_F1 moves up, E_F2 moves down, until they're **level**.

### M1: True or false

1. At zero bias, E_F is flat across a junction.
2. At zero bias, no carriers cross the junction.
3. The internal field is caused by the fixed dopant ions.
4. Far from the junction, the bands look the same as before contact.
5. Electrons flow from the material with the lower E_F to the one with the higher E_F.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1. **True.** (§3)
2. **False.** Carriers cross both ways. Drift cancels diffusion. (§4)
3. **True.** (§4)
4. **True.** Rule 2 in the big idea.
5. **False.** The other way. High E_F to low E_F. (§3)

</details>

### M2: Multiple choice

Two pieces of n-Si are joined. One has N_D = 10¹⁵, the other N_D = 10¹⁸. At contact:

- A. Nothing happens. Both are n-type.
- B. Electrons flow from the 10¹⁸ side to the 10¹⁵ side.
- C. Electrons flow from the 10¹⁵ side to the 10¹⁸ side.
- D. Holes flow from the 10¹⁸ side to the 10¹⁵ side.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.**

The 10¹⁸ side has E_F higher (closer to E_C). Electrons flow from high E_F to low E_F.

Also: there are more electrons on the 10¹⁸ side, so they diffuse to the 10¹⁵ side. Same answer.

From: §3, §4

</details>

### M3: Explain

In your own words, why do the bands bend near a junction but stay flat far away from it?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Diffusion leaves fixed ions behind only **near** the junction. That's where the charge is, so that's where the field is.

Tilt = field (Topic 7). So the bands bend only where the ions are, and are flat everywhere else.

From: §4

</details>

### M4: Sketch

n-Si with E_C − E_F = 0.2 eV is joined to p-Si with E_F − E_V = 0.1 eV. Sketch the band diagram at zero bias and label all distances.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

- Flat E_F.
- Far left (n): E_C 0.2 eV above E_F. E_V 1.12 eV below E_C.
- Far right (p): E_V 0.1 eV below E_F. E_C 1.12 eV above E_V.
- The p-side bands are higher. The step in E_C from n to p is 1.12 − 0.2 − 0.1 = **0.82 eV**. (That's eV₀, Topic 10.)
- Smooth S-bend at the junction.

From: §5, drawing checklist

</details>

### M5: Explain

Why is the p-side of the band diagram higher than the n-side?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Electrons diffused from n to p. The p-side gained negative charge.

Electron potential energy is high where the charge is negative (Topic 7: bands high at the − end).

So the p-side bands sit higher.

From: §4

</details>
