### Q1 (Tutorial Ch1 Q1)
Sketch a 2D Si lattice of 9 atoms with their valence electrons at **T = 0 K**. How does it change at **T = 300 K**?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

```
T = 0 K                         T = 300 K
 Si : Si : Si                   Si : Si : Si
 ..   ..   ..                   ..   ..   ..
 Si : Si : Si                   Si : Si  o Si      ● ← free e⁻ (moves)
 ..   ..   ..                   ..   ..   ..       o = hole h⁺ (empty spot)
 Si : Si : Si                   Si : Si : Si
(":" and ".." = 2-electron bond)
```
- At **0 K**: every valence electron is locked in a bond. No free carriers, so it's an insulator.
- At **300 K**: thermal energy breaks a few bonds. Each break gives **1 free e⁻ + 1 hole**, so n = p.
</details>

### Q2 (Tutorial Ch1 Q2)
Repeat the T > 0 K sketch for (i) **p-type** and (ii) **n-type** Si.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

```
(i) p-type: centre = B          (ii) n-type: centre = P
 Si : Si : Si                     Si : Si : Si
 ..   ..   ..                     ..   ..   ..
 Si : B  o Si   o = hole h⁺       Si : P  : Si   ● ← extra free e⁻
 ..   ..   ..                     ..   ..   ..
 Si : Si : Si                     Si : Si : Si
```
- **B** has 3 valence e⁻, so one bond is short an electron → **hole**. B becomes a fixed **B⁻** ion once the hole leaves.
- **P** has 5 valence e⁻, so 4 bond and **1 spare → free electron**. P becomes a fixed **P⁺** ion.
- Because T > 0, you can still draw one thermal e⁻/h⁺ pair, but the dopant carrier dominates.
</details>

### Q3 (Tutorial Ch1 Q3)
Holes in semiconductors are:
- i. Voids in the lattice where the atom is missing
- ii. A positively charged carrier that can freely roam the lattice at T = 300 K
- iii. A proton in the nucleus

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**ii.** A hole is a missing *bond electron*, not a missing atom (i) and not a proton (iii). It moves as neighbouring electrons fill it, and it acts like a +e charge.
</details>

### Quick-fire (from the lecture slides)

**a.** How many electrons are free at T = 0 K?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>0.</details>

**b.** Intrinsic Si at 300 K has 1.45×10¹⁰ cm⁻³ electrons. What's the hole density?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The same, 1.45×10¹⁰ cm⁻³. Every broken bond makes one of each.</details>

**c.** Why does a semiconductor's resistance *fall* as T rises, while a metal's rises?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>More heat breaks more bonds, so there are more carriers. A metal's carrier count is basically fixed, and extra heat only adds scattering.</details>

**d.** What group must a dopant be in to make n-type Si? What about p-type?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>n-type: group V (e.g. P, 5 e⁻). p-type: group III (e.g. B, 3 e⁻).</details>

**e.** Do holes exist in a single isolated Si atom?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>No. Holes only exist in a lattice, as a missing bond electron.</details>
